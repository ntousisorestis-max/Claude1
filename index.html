01

Start with problems, not "ideas"

Scrolling lists of "100 app ideas" is the slowest way to find yours — every idea on those lists is generic because it was written for everyone. Instead, look backward at your own last few weeks. What did you get annoyed at? What did you Google, then Google again a different way because the first result was bad? What do you or people around you currently solve with a messy spreadsheet, a group chat, or a sticky note?

An app idea is just a solution to one of those things, running on a screen you already carry.

02

Mine what you already know

You don't need a completely novel idea — average execution on something you understand beats a brilliant idea in a niche you know nothing about. The sweet spot is the overlap between three lists: what you're skilled at, what you're genuinely interested in, and a group of people whose day-to-day you understand well (because you're one of them).

03

Shrink it before you validate it

The most common idea-killer isn't a bad idea — it's an idea that's too big to start. Before you validate or build anything, cut it down to the smallest version that still delivers the core value. A useful test: can you describe it as "one input in, one useful result out"? If not, it's still too big.

04

Validate with conversations, not code

Before writing a line of code, talk to 5–10 people who'd actually be the user. Ask about their current behavior and pain — not whether they'd use your app (people are bad at predicting their own future behavior, and polite about it). The strongest signal isn't enthusiasm, it's evidence: are they already duct-taping together a workaround (a spreadsheet, a shared doc, a group chat) to deal with this? That means they'll pay with time or money for a real solution.

05

Pick the shape, then the stack

Almost every app idea is one of four shapes: Content (you teach or inform), Tool (you solve one task), Community (you connect people), or Portfolio/Business (you represent yourself or sell something). Once you know the shape, decisions about platform, design, and features get far easier — you're no longer choosing from infinite options, just the handful that fit that shape.
